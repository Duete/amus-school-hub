Item 1: Copper Processing — Electrochemistry & Thermochemistry

A metal-processing company in Uganda produces copper components used in electrical equipment. The company is reviewing its production processes to reduce energy costs while maintaining the required output. Its operations involve electrochemical cells, electrolytic recovery of copper, and chemical reduction of copper compounds.

During a school-industry study programme, Senior Six learners joined the company's technical team to assess the efficiency and suitability of these processes. In one section, engineers proposed an electrochemical cell made by connecting Zn2+/Zn and Cu2+/Cu half-cells under standard conditions. The cell is expected to supply electrical energy to monitoring equipment that requires a potential difference of at least 1.00 V. The engineers need to establish the electrode reactions, direction of electron flow, overall cell reaction and cell notation, and determine the electrical energy available from the cell.

Half-cell E° / V
Cu2+(aq) + 2e- → Cu(s) +0.34
Zn2+(aq) + 2e- → Zn(s) -0.76
Ag+(aq) + e- → Ag(s) +0.80

In another section, copper is recovered from aqueous copper(II) sulphate by electrolysis using inert electrodes. During each operating cycle, a current of 19.3 A is passed for 2.00 hours. The company expects at least 22.0 g of copper to be deposited per cycle. Production records showed that changes in the current supplied, duration of electrolysis, and concentration of Cu2+ ions in the electrolyte affected the operation of the recovery unit. The engineers therefore need to understand how these factors affect the process when selecting operating conditions that give adequate copper output without unnecessary energy use.

Cu2+(aq) + 2e- → Cu(s)     Ar(Cu) = 63.5

The company is also considering an alternative process in which copper(II) oxide is reduced using hydrogen:

CuO(s) + H2(g) → Cu(s) + H2O(l)

The technical team wants to establish whether this reaction absorbs or releases heat, determine the associated energy change, and assess its implications for the company's efforts to reduce energy consumption.

Substance ΔHf° / kJ mol-1
CuO(s) -155
H2O(l) -286
Cu(s) 0
H2(g) 0

As a member of the learner team, you have been asked to use the information provided and relevant chemical principles to help the company make appropriate technical decisions.

Task:

(a) Analyse the electrochemical and electrolysis information, explaining how the identified operating factors affect copper recovery, and assess the suitability and effectiveness of the proposed processes.

(b) Evaluate the thermochemical information and recommend an appropriate production decision for the company.

Item 2: Thermochemistry — Battery Systems & Heat Generation

Thermochemistry deals with heat changes during chemical processes, which is essential for controlling temperatures and managing thermal safety in extreme environments.

An environmental engineering team is setting up an automatic weather station in a very cold zone. The station needs an emergency backup battery to power a radio transmitter so it can send weather data back to headquarters. To keep the radio running without losing power too quickly, the battery reaction of the chosen battery must release a minimum of 300.0 kJ/mol of electrical energy and all conditions that can affect it kept constant. Any battery that gives out less electrical energy will cause the radio transmitter to turn off too early. To find the best option, the engineering group builds two different battery cells in the laboratory.

Battery system 1:

Zn(s) + Cu2+(aq) → Zn2+(aq) + Cu(s)

Battery system 2:

Zn(s) + 2Ag+(aq) → Zn2+(aq) + 2Ag(s)

Engineers noticed that due to the extremely low temperatures, the liquid inside the batteries had frozen and would not conduct electricity. To fix this, engineers installed a small heater inside the battery box. This heater triggers the combustion of carbon in the presence of hydrogen gas, assuming all other factors are taken into consideration, which chemically yields an intense amount of heat that melts the ice inside the battery to restore the liquid electrolyte back to a functional state.

C(s) + 2H2(g) → CH4(g)

If a freeze warning occurs, the station's computer automatically feeds exactly 2.0 moles of carbon into the heater. For this safety system to work and melt the ice, these 2.0 moles must release at least 140.0 kJ of total heat energy. The team has the following known data equations from laboratory records:

Equation Enthalpy change of the reaction
C(s) + O2(g) → CO2(g) -393.5 kJ/mol
H2(g) + ½O2(g) → H2O(l) -285.8 kJ/mol
CH4(g) + 2O2(g) → CO2(g) + H2O(l) -890.3 kJ/mol

Laboratory Data and Constants Pack

Half cell Reduction potential
Zn2+(aq) + 2e- → Zn(s) -0.76 V
Cu2+(aq) + 2e- → Cu(s) +0.34 V
Ag+(aq) + e- → Ag(s) +0.80 V

Task:

(a) Analyse the data to determine which battery system would be better and if the heater provided the required amount of heat.

(b) Explain factors that would favour sufficient production of heat to melt the ice.

(c) Explain other applications of the chemical systems used.

Section A (Number 2)
Item 1: Organic Acid Identity & Buffer Formulation

A food-processing company in Uganda manufactures fruit-based products that require controlled acidity for acceptable taste and shelf life. The company uses organic acids as acidity regulators and prepares buffer solutions to maintain the pH of its products during processing and storage.

During a school-industry study programme, Senior Six learners joined the company's quality-control team when a consignment of an organic acid, HA, arrived with an incomplete label. Before using the acid, the company needed to confirm its identity, determine the amount of acid available and establish whether it could be used to prepare a suitable buffer. The laboratory team established that HA is a saturated monocarboxylic acid with the general formula CnH2nO2. To determine its molecular formula, 10.0 cm3 of HA vapour was mixed with 70.0 cm3 of oxygen and completely burnt. After cooling to room temperature, the gaseous mixture occupied 50.0 cm3. When the mixture was passed through aqueous potassium hydroxide, its volume decreased to 30.0 cm3. All gas volumes were measured under the same temperature and pressure conditions.

CnH2nO2(g) + ((3n-2)/2) O2(g) → n CO2(g) + n H2O(l)

In another test, 25.0 cm3 of a solution prepared from the consignment required 20.00 cm3 of 0.1 M NaOH for complete neutralisation. The company has 500 dm3 of this acid solution available for production.

HA(aq) + NaOH(aq) → NaA(aq) + H2O(l)

The company intends to use part of the acid to prepare a buffer for a fruit product whose required pH is 4.60–4.80. A technician proposed mixing 0.1 M of HA with 0.080 M of NaA and making the mixture up to 1.00 dm3. For the acid identified from the combustion investigation:

Ka = 1.8 × 10-5 mol dm-3

During processing, small quantities of acid may enter the product from fruit ingredients. The technical team explained that an effective buffer should resist large changes in pH when small amounts of acid or alkali are introduced. The company therefore needs to understand how the relative amounts of HA and A- influence the buffer pH and why the buffer is more effective when appreciable quantities of both components are present.

As a member of the learner team, you have been asked to use the laboratory information and relevant chemical principles to advise the company.

Task:

(a) Analyse the eudiometric and stoichiometric information to establish the identity and concentration of HA, and determine the amount of HA available in the production batch.

(b) Evaluate the proposed buffer formulation, explaining how its composition controls pH and assess its suitability and application in the company's fruit-processing operation.

Item 2: Buffer Formulations & Solubility Equilibria

Clinical trials showed that small changes in pH accelerated decomposition of the active ingredient, reducing its therapeutic value. To improve stability, chemists needed to develop formulations to use. However, during long-term storage, some batches developed white precipitates that blocked intravenous tubing. Further investigations showed that calcium ions in the formulation reacted with carbonate ions introduced through poor storage conditions, forming insoluble calcium carbonate.

To identify the most suitable formulation, two of them were prepared using ethanoic acid (CH3COOH) and sodium ethanoate (CH3COONa). Each solution was prepared by dissolving the stated masses in distilled water and making the solution up to 250 cm3.

Item 4: Steam Distillation & Molar Mass Determination

A pharmaceutical company uses steam distillation to purify some aromatic organic substances obtained from plant extracts. During a study visit, a group of Senior Five Chemistry learners were attached to the quality-control section, where they were challenged to investigate the purification of one of the substances. The quality-control officer explained that the success of the process depends on conditions such as temperature, pressure and the vapour pressures of the substances involved. She also told the learners that the company uses steam distillation mainly for suitable organic substances with molar masses between 100 and 300 g mol-1, and that determining the composition and molar mass of the substance was important in identifying it and assessing its suitability for the process.

At a pressure of 101 kPa, the learners were given the following data from the purification process:

Temperature T / °C 92 94 96 98 100
Vapour pressure of organic substance / kPa 6 8 12 15 17
Vapour pressure of water / kPa 74 80 88 94 101

During the experiment, 1.60 g of water and 1.10 g of the organic substance were collected in the distillate. Further analysis showed that the organic substance contained 77.42% carbon, 15.05% nitrogen, and the remainder hydrogen by mass. The quality-control officer explained that establishing the molecular formula is important in establishing the identity of the sample.

Task:

(a) Explain how the factors above determine the effectiveness of steam distillation in this process.

Visiting chemistry learners have been told that one production line uses ethene to manufacture butan-1-ol. A second production line intends to make a monomer required for nylon 6,6. The company proposes converting benzene to hexanedioic acid through a multistep process. The production team has challenged the learners to determine the appropriate reagents, conditions, structures of intermediates, and equations for both synthetic pathways.

The packaging section also converts ethene to poly(ethene) and benzene to nitrobenzene. The learners are tasked with suggesting the reaction pathways that might be involved.

Quality-control staff have observed that poly(ethene) is flexible and insoluble in water, while nylon 6,6 has high tensile strength and low solubility in water. Management wants these properties explained from the structures, bonding and intermolecular forces present in the polymers and related to their uses.

The company is also concerned about the persistence of discarded plastics, safe handling of aromatic chemicals and the environmental effects associated with polymer manufacture and disposal. As a Senior Six chemistry learner attached to the production team, you have been requested to assist in evaluating the proposed processes.

Tasks:

(a) Analyse the proposed synthetic pathways and represent the transformations using appropriate equations.

(b) Account for the formation of poly(ethene) and nitrobenzene using appropriate reaction mechanisms.

(c) Relate the properties and uses of the polymers to their structures and advise the company on their responsible production, use and disposal.

Item 3: 1,2-Dichloroethane Fumigant & Propanone Solvent

An agrochemical manufacturing plant located at Luzira Industrial Area synthesises a fumigant used in post-harvest grain preservation. The active ingredient in this formulation is 1,2-dichloroethane and is dissolved in high-purity propanone as the industrial solvent to completely dissolve the pesticide.

During a routine supply audit, a new quality assurance intern raised two management questions to the technical team.

The intern noted that instead of disposing of the raw expired batches of 1,2-dichloroethane pesticide, the factory could study how it is chemically transformed into ethyne.

The intern discovered an unlabeled container in the solvent storage bay. To verify whether it was the required propanone solvent, they suggested sampling the liquid and testing how it reacts with Brady's reagent (2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine).

Furthermore, due to shortages in supply, the research division is investigating green chemistry pathways to make both components locally from locally available raw materials. They want to map out how the pesticide (1,2-dichloroethane) can be derived from chloroethane, and the solvent (propanone) can be efficiently produced starting from ethanol.

Understanding the boiling points of the components and their raw materials, as well as evaluating the use of propanone as the industrial solvent, is important.

Task: Make a report with guidance on the issues raised.

Item 4: Propanone to Perspex Monomer & Ethene

A certain company that manufactures alcohols and polymer-based packaging materials has recently expanded its production.

The different production units use hydrocarbons obtained from petroleum as raw materials to manufacture the alcohols and polymers. Due to increased production, there is a scarcity of raw materials, and they have been advised that Methyl-2-methylpropenoate a raw material for Perspex, and ethene, a raw material for the plastic polymer polythene, can each be made using propanone as the starting material. In order to be able to control likely explosions, the company is seeking to understand how the reactions for the formation of polythene from its raw material and 1-bromopropan-2-ol from propene take place.

The company has been conditioned to effectively manage the disposal of waste materials from the plastic polymer, which is said to be non-biodegradable, and this has prompted them to seek to understand why it behaves this way and evaluate its use. The company lacks qualified personnel to help them do all the above and has advertised vacancies for those who can help. Your teacher, who read the advert, has provided you with this information.

Task: As a chemistry student, make a detailed write-up you would use to try to help this company.

Item 5: Propan-1-ol & Ethyne to Industrial Products

Many useful substances are obtained from organic compounds and are often converted into other useful substances through different chemical reactions. Recently, a chemical manufacturing company which uses propan-1-ol and ethyne as raw materials to produce products required by their customers complained about their different behaviors when heated. The quality assurance team advised that the boiling points of the raw materials and the products formed should always be analysed to avoid likely explosions, but the company lacked expertise.

A chemistry learner who visited the factory during a science innovation exhibition became interested in how these simple organic compounds could be converted into more useful industrial products. During discussions with the industrial chemists, the learner discovered that the company was particularly interested in improving the conversion of propan-1-ol into ethanol and ethyne into 1-phenyl ethanol because the products were needed in the manufacture of other products. The company also needed to understand how the reactions for the formation of benzene sulphonic acid from benzene and ethanal oxime from ethanal occur. However, the learner was not fully sure about how to do this. The learner has therefore approached you for help.

Task: As a chemistry learner;

a) Help the company to analyse the boiling points of their raw materials and products.

b) Help the learner to provide information on how the above reactions occur and how the products needed by the company can be made.

c) Evaluate the industrial importance and safety concerns associated with the raw materials in the above industrial processes.

Item 6: Agro-processing & Chemical Manufacturing Enterprise

A cooperative of young entrepreneurs in Uganda plans to establish a small agroprocessing and chemical manufacturing enterprise. The enterprise intends to produce and market a range of products, including: a fruit-flavored beverage made from locally grown pineapples and mangoes, a fragrance additive for soaps and cosmetics, a cleaning solvent for industrial and laboratory use, and a biofuel additive to improve fuel combustion efficiency.

Before production begins, the entrepreneurs must select suitable organic compounds and production methods. The compounds considered for use are:

  • Ethene (C2H4)
  • Ethanol (C2H5OH)
  • Ethanoic acid (CH3COOH)
  • Propan-1-ol (C3H7OH)
  • Ethyl ethanoate (CH3COOC2H5)

During preliminary trials, they observe that some liquids evaporate quickly while others remain liquid for long periods, and they wonder why. The cooperative also reacts benzene with concentrated nitric acid and sulfuric acid, and propan-1-ol with ethanoic acid in the presence of sulphuric acid, but lacks information on how the reactions actually occur.

They also discovered that several products can be made from the same starting materials, and they consider preparing propyl benzoate and propanone each from ethene but lack expertise.

The cooperative also lacks understanding of the suitability of different organic compounds for specific intended applications in the production of the different products.

Task:

a) Explain the differences in the evaporation behaviour of the compounds considered for use.

b) Help the cooperative solve their challenges about the reactions and formation of the intended products.

c) Assess the suitability of one of the compounds for use in the production of a specified product the company intends to make.

Item 7: Agrochemicals — Green Chemistry Proposals

An Agrochemicals industry manufactures herbicides, insecticides and plastic irrigation pipes used by commercial farmers. The industry recently partnered with researchers from a certain University to develop environmentally safer products after several farming communities reported contamination of groundwater and accumulation of plastic wastes in agricultural fields.

The research team advised that some petroleum-derived chemicals should be replaced with other environmentally friendly compounds. They proposed the preparation of N-phenyl ethanamide and 2-methylpropan-2-ol from ethyne, and a study of how the reactions leading to the formation of polychloroethene from chloroethene and propyne from 1,2-dibromopropane occur.

Laboratory trials also showed that ethene, benzene, methylbenzene and phenol initially used possessed markedly different boiling points. The research team also proposed a thorough assessment of polychloroethene use before its manufacture commences. Your teacher wants to know if you can work as part of this research team and has decided to test you.

Task: Analyse the differences in the boiling points of the compounds observed during the laboratory trials. Make a detailed analysis of the proposals made by the research team.
